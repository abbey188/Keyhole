What Keyhole does, and what it does not

Every tool for sharing has edges. Here are ours, and what Keyhole does about each one.

The edge
A screen can be photographed

No app can stop a camera pointed at a screen.

What Keyhole does
Names the viewer on every page

Their name and visit time sit across the page, and the visit is in the log. A photo carries their name.

The edge
What someone read, they know

Ending access cannot make a person forget.

What Keyhole does
Stops the next look, at once

The next open fails. Re-locking the safe changes its keys and erases the stored copy.

The edge
Your passkey is your key

Whoever holds your unlocked device holds your safe.

What Keyhole does
Uses the lock you already trust

Your face, fingerprint or device PIN. Passkeys sync across your own devices through your platform.

The edge
A claim is your statement

A machine reading your payslip is not your employer signing it.

What Keyhole does
Shows how each claim was made

Read on your device, reviewed and signed by you, and graded. Claims signed by the issuer are next.

The edge
AI can be wrong

The hardware report proves where the model ran, not that it is right.

What Keyhole does
Cites the page behind every answer

Anyone with access can check the answer against the page. The hardware's current patch level is the next check we add.

The edge
One release service today

It cannot read files, but it decides when to hand out a key half.

What Keyhole does
Checks the chain before every release

It refuses anything the chain does not show as open. Splitting it across independent services is next.

Where it runs. Keyhole runs on NEAR testnet. The contract has no keys and its code is public, so anyone can check what it does. An external audit is the next step.
