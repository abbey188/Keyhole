Who can see what

Keyhole is built so that the people who run it cannot read what you keep in it. Here is every part of the system and what it can see.

The parts

PartSees your filesWhat keeps it in check
Your deviceYesThe only place your files are readable. Keys come from your passkey and are never stored.
StorageNoHolds sealed pieces only. Every read is checked against the record on the chain.
The contractNoHas no keys after launch, so nobody can change its rules or move a safe.
Release serviceNoHolds half of each shared key and hands it out only after the visit is recorded on the chain.
Private AIWhat you chooseGets only the lines you pick, encrypted to hardware that proves what it runs.

Recorded before opened

A link never opens anything on its own. Each visit follows the same order.

01The visitor opens the link. Half of the key is in the link itself.
02The visit is written to the chain and becomes final.
03The release service reads the chain itself and checks the access is still open, in time and in scope.
04Only then does it send the other half of the key.
05The page is decrypted on the visitor's screen, with their name across it.

Public and private

On the chain
The safe's number and owner. When access was given and used. The result of a hand over.
Only you
File contents, names and sizes. What a visitor looked at. Your claims until you share them.

Keyhole keeps contents confidential and access accountable. It does not hide who owns a safe.

Private AI

Before any text leaves your device, the app checks the model's hardware report. It confirms the report is fresh, the signing key sits inside genuine Intel hardware, debug mode is off, and the GPU passes NVIDIA's check. Then it encrypts the text to that key.

The report proves where the model runs. It does not prove the answer is right, so every answer links back to the page it came from.
